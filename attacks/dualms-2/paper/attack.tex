\begin{abstract}
We analyze the rounded construction in \emph{DualMS 2.0}, IACR ePrint
2026/1941. Its ordinary verifier reconstructs a rounded commitment $u$, adds a
signature hint $h$, hashes $u+h$, and checks a joint norm on $(z,2^{\nu_w}h)$.
For any aggregate public key and attacker-chosen fresh message, choose a
rounded commitment $w$, query $c=\Hsig(w,\mu,\mathit{aggpk})$, set all response
values $z,r,\bar r$ to zero, and choose $h=w-u$ for
$u=\roundnu{-c2^{\nu_b}\mathit{aggpk}}{\nu_w}$. The verifier reconstructs
$w$ exactly, while every response norm except the scaled hint vanishes.

At the advertised 32-signer rounded row, the remaining condition is
$\norm{h}^2\leq 138330$ for a uniform $h\in R_{31}^{7}$. A conservative
Berry--Esseen calculation gives success probability at least
$0.0300039691$ per distinct $\Hsig$ query, expected work at most $33.329$
queries, and success probability at least $0.950996$ within 99 queries. At the
advertised 1,024-signer row, $h\in R_{15}^{10}$ and every canonical hint has
$\norm{h}^2\leq125440$, whereas the printed bound permits $329464658$.
One $\Hsig$ query therefore suffices for every candidate commitment.

A prospectively sealed primary experiment used exact moduli selected by the
paper-linked parameter artifact. A general ordinary verifier accepted
fresh-message forgeries at both full rows with zero signing-oracle queries, using 10
and one $\Hsig$ queries respectively; all retained negative controls rejected.
A separate hostile review reconstructed both equations and bounds and returned
PASS. A prospectively sealed fresh-context implementation independently
accepted both exact rows with zero signing-oracle queries, using four and one
$\Hsig$ queries, respectively. It directly absorbed the complete registration
list in $\Hagg$, retained the list witnesses, and satisfied all sealed control
criteria in 28.16 seconds with 252,432 KiB peak RSS on one worker. The paper
supplies no normative final tuple codec, and the author Go artifact uses
different NTT-friendly moduli, so the result is a mathematical paper-level
random-oracle attack rather than a byte-exact source-conformance claim. The
public standard-library reproducer uses a declared direct length-delimited
codec. This result is classified as a verified practical design attack.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

DualMS 2.0 is a two-round same-message lattice multisignature that applies
modulus rounding to the aggregate public key and aggregate signature to reduce
communication~\cite{dualms2}. Its advertised rounded rows cover 32 and 1,024
signers and report underlying MSIS and MLWE costs near the 128-bit target. The
rounding optimization introduces an explicit hint $h$: signature aggregation
sets $h$ to the difference between the rounded aggregate commitment and a
verifier-computable rounded target.

The ordinary verifier does not establish that the hint came from honest
aggregation. Instead, it permits the hint to share one Euclidean bound with
the Gaussian response $z$. Setting every response to zero removes all secret
and commitment-oracle terms. The attacker can then use the hint to translate
the public reconstruction to any selected rounded commitment. The hash
challenge is obtained in the usual way by querying the public random oracle on
that commitment.

The decisive question is whether the hint fits the printed norm bound. For the
32-signer row it does with constant probability. For the 1,024-signer row the
bound is so loose that every canonical hint fits by a factor above 2,600 in
squared-norm space. The resulting signatures use zero signing-oracle queries
and verify on attacker-chosen messages that have never been signed.

\paragraph{Contribution and originality boundary.}
We give the exact attack equation, analyze both advertised rounded parameter

\subsection{Security game}

The MS-UF-CMA game gives the adversary public parameters and one honest public
key. It permits an output list $L^*$ containing that key and accepts a valid
$(L^*,\mu^*,\mathit{AggSig}^*)$ when $(L^*,\mu^*)$ was never submitted to the
first-round signing oracle. No proof of possession or KeyGen proof is imposed
on the remaining registered keys. A forgery after zero signing-oracle queries for a
list containing the challenge key is therefore a direct game win.

The algebra below works for every aggregate public key, so it does not depend
on a specially structured corrupt-key list. The primary 32-signer run used 32
honest KeyGen outputs. The primary 1,024-signer run and public
reproducer use one honest challenge key and distinct adversarially registered
keys, exactly within the printed game.

\section{Rounded verification}
\label{sec:verify}

Let $A\in R_q^{m\times\ell}$ and let the rounded aggregate public key be
$\widetilde t_{\nu_b}\in R_{q_b}^{m}$. An aggregate signature is
\[
  (c,\widetilde z,\widetilde r,\widetilde{\boldsymbol r},h)
  \in \mathcal C\times R_q^\ell\times R_q\times R_q^\ell\times R_{q_w}^m,
\]
where $\mathcal C$ consists of degree-256 polynomials with exactly $\kappa=60$
coefficients in $\{\pm1\}$.

Given message $\mu$, the ordinary verifier samples
\[
  [b,B_{\mathrm{mat}}]=\Hcom(\widetilde t_{\nu_b},\mu)
\]
and computes
\begin{align*}
  u=\roundnu{
      A\widetilde z
      -c2^{\nu_b}\widetilde t_{\nu_b}
      +\widetilde r b
      +B_{\mathrm{mat}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol r}
    }{\nu_w},
  \qquad
  \widetilde w_{\nu_w}=u+h.
\end{align*}
It accepts precisely when
\begin{align}
  \Hsig(\widetilde w_{\nu_w},\mu,\widetilde t_{\nu_b})&=c,
    \label{eq:hash}\\
  \norm{(\widetilde z,2^{\nu_w}h)}&\leq \mathcal B,
    \label{eq:joint}\\
  \norm{\widetilde r}&\leq \mathcal B'_n,
    \label{eq:rbound}\\
  \norm{\widetilde{\boldsymbol r}}&\leq \mathcal B''_n.
    \label{eq:rbarbound}
\end{align}
The paper's $\mathcal B$ is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal B={}&\sigma\sqrt{2n\ell N}
 +\sqrt{mN}\left(
    \sigma\sqrt{2n}+\eta+\kappa2^{\nu_b-1}+2^{\nu_w}
  \right).
 \label{eq:B}
\end{align}

The hint has no independent provenance or shortness check. Its only
restriction is its contribution to Equation~\eqref{eq:joint}.

\section{Public-key-only forgery}
\label{sec:attack}

\begin{theorem}[Hint-slack forgery]
Fix any rounded DualMS 2.0 aggregate public key
$\widetilde t_{\nu_b}$ and any attacker-chosen message $\mu$. For any
$w\in R_{q_w}^{m}$, query
\[
  c=\Hsig(w,\mu,\widetilde t_{\nu_b}),
\]
set
\[
  \widetilde z=0,\qquad \widetilde r=0,\qquad
  \widetilde{\boldsymbol r}=0,
\]
and compute
\[
  u=\roundnu{-c2^{\nu_b}\widetilde t_{\nu_b}}{\nu_w},
  \qquad h=w-u\pmod{q_w}.
\]
The resulting aggregate signature passes the hash equation and the two
separate response bounds. It is accepted exactly when
\[
  2^{2\nu_w}\norm{h}^2\leq \mathcal B^2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With all three responses zero, the verifier's $A\widetilde z$,
$\widetilde r b$, and $B_{\mathrm{mat}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol r}$ terms vanish.
It therefore recomputes the same $u$ as the adversary. Adding $h=w-u$ gives
$\widetilde w_{\nu_w}=w$, so Equation~\eqref{eq:hash} holds by the adversary's
ordinary $\Hsig$ query. Equations~\eqref{eq:rbound} and
\eqref{eq:rbarbound} hold with norm zero. Equation~\eqref{eq:joint} contains
only the scaled hint and reduces to the stated inequality.
\end{proof}

The forger never calls either signing oracle and never uses a secret. The
message is therefore fresh in the exact game. This is not a zero-total-query
attack: the adversary must make the public $\Hsig$ queries stated below.
$\Hcom$ remains part of ordinary verification, but its outputs are multiplied
by zero.

\subsection{Codec independence}

The paper defines the random oracles on bitstrings but does not fix final tuple
framing. This prevents a byte-for-byte paper known-answer test. It does not
affect the theorem: the forger calls the same public
$\Hsig(w,\mu,\widetilde t_{\nu_b})$ interface as the verifier and copies the
returned challenge unchanged. No collision, ambiguous parse, chosen digest,
or special challenge value is needed. Any deterministic injective completion
of the fixed-shape tuple gives the same attack equation.

\section{Concrete analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{The 32-signer row}

Independent 100-digit arithmetic gives
\begin{align*}
  \sigma &=33910694.7761986003223\ldots,\\
  \mathcal B&=24959699776.3818219481\ldots,\\
  \left\lfloor(\mathcal B/2^{26})^2\right\rfloor&=138330.
\end{align*}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform hint]
In the ideal random-oracle experiment, if the attacker chooses $w$ uniformly
from $R_{31}^{7}$, then $h=w-u(c)$ is uniform in $R_{31}^{7}$ for any fixed
aggregate key, where $c=\Hsig(w,\mu,\mathit{aggpk})$ and $u$ is any
deterministic function of $c$ and public data.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a uniformly selected $w$, the ideal random-oracle value $c$ is uniform and
independent of $w$. For every fixed $h_0$,
\[
 \Pr[h=h_0]
 =\sum_c \Pr[c]\Pr[w=h_0+u(c)\mid c]
 =\frac{1}{|R_{31}^{7}|}.
\]
Thus $h$ is uniform over the product space.
\end{proof}

There are $7\cdot256=1792$ independent centered coefficients, each uniform on
$\{-15,\ldots,15\}$. If $X$ is the square of one coefficient, direct summation
gives
\[
  \mathbb E[X]=80,\qquad
  \mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(X)=5104,\qquad
  \mathbb E[|X-80|^3]=\frac{15494144}{31}.
\]
The sum has mean $143360$ and standard deviation
$3024.29628178\ldots$. At threshold 138330, the standardized value without a
continuity correction is $-1.66319683\ldots$. The normal cumulative
probability is $0.04813652\ldots$, and a Berry--Esseen bound with conservative
constant 0.56 contributes error at most $0.01813255\ldots$. Hence
\[
  \Pr[\norm{h}^2\leq138330]
  \geq 0.0300039691166414.
\]
The conservative expected-query upper bound is $33.3289238$, and 99 distinct
oracle inputs succeed with probability at least
\[
  1-(1-0.0300039691166414)^{99}
  \geq 0.9509966510.
\]

\subsection{The 1,024-signer row}

For the larger row, independent arithmetic gives
\begin{align*}
  \sigma &=1195768501.04620372663\ldots,\\
  \mathcal B&=4872415274260.4413014171\ldots,\\
  \left\lfloor(\mathcal B/2^{28})^2\right\rfloor&=329464658.
\end{align*}
Here $q_w=15$, so a canonical centered hint coefficient has magnitude at most
7. Across $10\cdot256=2560$ coefficients,
\[
  \max_{h\in R_{15}^{10}}\norm{h}^2
  =2560\cdot7^2
  =125440
  <329464658.
\]

\begin{corollary}
At the advertised rounded 1,024-signer row, every canonical hint satisfies the
joint $z/h$ bound when $z=0$. One $\Hsig$ query therefore produces a forgery
for every selected rounded commitment.
\end{corollary}

The squared-bound ratio is above 2,626. This row needs no hint grind or
probability estimate.

\section{Related technique and history}

We located no previous public attack against DualMS 2.0, including either advertised rounded Table-6 profile (n=32 and n=1024), in searches completed on 16 October 2026. The closest earlier public analogue we located is NGCC CS finding sign-07-2, where a differently structured verifier permits a hint to absorb reconstruction terms; our result targets DualMS 2.0's printed verifier and parameter bounds and does not rely on CS's challenge-sign blindness or support grind. We make no claim that the general hint-absorption technique is novel.

The sealed final-history recheck covered exact-title, alias, identifier,
general-web, official ACM/ePrint, author/repository, GitHub issue, pull-request,
advisory, commit, forum, and scholarly-index checks. It returned
\texttt{PASS / NO\_PRIOR\_PUBLIC\_EXACT\_TARGET\_ATTACK\_LOCATED} with mandatory
related-technique attribution retained. The target had been approved only 34
days earlier, its conference presentation was still in the future, scholarly
indexing was incomplete, and the live-search seal did not retain raw endpoint
responses. The result is dated and bounded and cannot exclude private,
deleted, unpublished, or unindexed work.

The closest earlier public analogue located is NGCC CS finding sign-07-2,
published 21 September 2026~\cite{ngcccs}. In CS, challenge-sign blindness and
the absence of an independent hint bound permit zero responses and a free hint
that absorbs reconstruction terms, followed by a support grind. The reported
full-size costs are about $2^{108.08}$, $2^{212.46}$, and $2^{394.18}$; the
complete demonstration is scaled. That result is not an attack on DualMS or
DualMS 2.0. The present forgeries do not require challenge-sign blindness or a
support grind, but the shared hint-absorption theme requires attribution.

The Del Pino--Prest patent EP4224785A1, with priority 3 February 2022 and
publication 9 August 2023, describes a verifier reconstruction hint and
explicitly warns that such a hint may break soundness, motivating independent
shortness checks~\cite{hintpatent}. It does not analyze the DualMS 2.0 verifier
or its concrete bound. Fukumitsu and Hasegawa provide earlier lattice
multisignature rounding lineage~\cite{fukumitsu}. We claim no novelty for the
general observation that hints require sound constraints.

\section{Impact, repair direction, and limitations}

The verified result is a direct fresh-message forgery under the ordinary
mathematical verifier and exact MS-UF-CMA list condition. It affects both
advertised rounded rows at practical work. The result does not recover a
signer's long-term secret, and it does not claim an attack on the unrounded
appendix construction.

A repair must prevent an attacker-chosen hint from translating an arbitrary
public reconstruction into a selected hashed commitment while remaining in
the acceptance region. One possible direction is an independently sound hint
bound; another is a redesign that proves consistency with an honestly formed
commitment. Any change must be analyzed together with correctness, rejection
sampling, concrete parameters, and the final serialization. We have not
validated a drop-in patch.

The following limitations delimit the result:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the paper body is hash-pinned and metadata-matched, but official PDF
        byte identity and a version ordinal are not established;
  \item exact integer moduli come from the paper-linked artifact because the
        body gives only bit lengths;
  \item the Go implementation uses different moduli, and no successful
        source-native execution is available;
  \item no normative final tuple, aggregate-key, or aggregate-signature codec
        or known-answer vector is published;
  \item the 32-signer probability is an ideal-random-oracle bound, while the
        1,024-signer statement is a deterministic bound over canonical hints;
  \item the final attack-history PASS is dated and bounded.
\end{itemize}

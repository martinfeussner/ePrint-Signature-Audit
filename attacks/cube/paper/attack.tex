\begin{abstract}
CUBE, proposed in IACR ePrint 2026/920, combines partially blind BBS
signatures with a Fiat--Shamir proof used by an on-chain credential verifier.
Its ordinary verifier accepts a proof
$\pi=(\bar A,\bar B,U,s,t,u_\gamma)$ when
\[
 e(\bar A,X)=e(\bar B,G_2),\qquad
 U+c\bar B=sC_J+t\bar A+u_\gamma H_\gamma.
\]
Neither the paper nor the pinned verifier requires the three proof points to
be nonidentity. Set $\bar A=\bar B=U=\OO$ and
$s=t=u_\gamma=0$. Both equations then hold for every challenge, disclosed
attribute vector, context, public key, and set of public bases. The resulting
proof needs no signature, witness, secret, issuance interaction, hash
collision, or malformed point.

In the paper's Sys-EUF-CMA game, the adversary makes zero signing-oracle
queries and submits this proof for any explicit attribute vector. The query
set is empty, so the vector is fresh and ordinary verification accepts. This
is a full-parameter, probability-one fresh-message forgery in the stated game.
An Arkworks harness using actual BN254 pairings and the pinned Solidity
Keccak-256 transcript accepted the candidate in both the deployed five-word
profile and a paper-complete profile containing five explicit attributes, a
nullifier, and an address term. Execution took 0.03 seconds and 2,048 KiB peak
resident memory. Honest proofs accepted, while four single-component
mutations and an honest-message mutation rejected.

A canonical read-only Sepolia replay made twelve public JSON-RPC calls. The
author-listed verifier accepted the zero proof on two input vectors, and a
historical-state simulation of the ordinary issuer accepted mint calldata
whose nine proof words alone were zeroed. Mutation and precommit-state
controls rejected. No transaction was sent, no private key was used, and this
evidence neither claims a broadcast forged mint nor an independent
source-to-runtime-bytecode match. A fresh-context independent reproduction
returned PASS. Generic BBS specifications had already required nonidentity
proof points; we claim no novelty for that rule or for the identity-element
technique.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and exact result}

Lai introduces CUBE (Credential Unlinkability using Blinded Elements), a
partially blind BBS credential framework intended to provide unlinkable
on-chain identity proofs and Soulbound Token minting~\cite{cube}. Its five
algorithms are $\Pi=(\Init,\Apply,\Sign,\Prove,\Verify)$. A user obtains a BBS
signature over public attributes and hidden address, nullifier, and blinding
components, randomizes that signature, and presents a Fiat--Shamir proof. The
on-chain verifier reconstructs the disclosed commitment, verifies two group
relations, consumes a nullifier, and permits minting.

The proof verifier omits a standard boundary condition: every received proof
point may be the group identity. At the identity, the two verification
relations lose all dependence on the alleged signature and witness. Our exact
claim is:

\begin{quote}
The ordinary CUBE verification equations in ePrint 2026/920 admit a
zero-signing-query Sys-EUF-CMA forgery at the full BN254 parameters. The
all-identity, all-zero proof is accepted for an attacker-chosen explicit
attribute vector that was never submitted to the signing oracle.
\end{quote}

The weakness is design-level. It follows directly from the printed equations,
uses valid group elements, and remains when the paper's address and nullifier
terms are included in $C_J$. The pinned Solidity source implements the same
two equations and explicitly represents the BN254 identity as $(0,0)$. The
attack does not use the source's separate omission of the address term, a
fixture key, a parser discrepancy, invalid-curve input, nonce failure, or any
accidental deployment condition.

``Zero-query'' refers to the issuance/signing oracle in the paper's security
game. An application may still require ordinary public workflow steps, such
as registering an unused nullifier. Those steps do not provide a signature or
credential witness. Our deployed evidence is deliberately narrower than an
exploit claim: it consists only of read-only calls and historical-state
simulations, not a broadcast mint.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We give the exact CUBE equations and game; prove the universal identity-proof
forgery; execute it with actual full-parameter BN254 pairings under both the
source and paper-complete profiles; exercise the author-listed Sepolia
addresses through a provider-bound, read-only simulation with state and
mutation controls; report hostile review and fresh-context independent
reproduction; place the result against generic BBS nonidentity-validation
prior art; and state repair directions and evidence limits.

\section{CUBE signatures, proofs, and security game}

\subsection{BBS commitment and randomized proof}

The construction instantiates the short BBS signature and its proof-of-
knowledge setting~\cite{bbs,tessaro-zhu}.
Let $\GOne,\GTwo,\GT$ be cyclic groups of prime order $p$, written additively
in the source groups, and let
\[
 e:\GOne\times\GTwo\longrightarrow\GT
\]
be a nondegenerate bilinear pairing. Let $G_1\in\GOne$ and
$G_2\in\GTwo$ be generators. A signer selects $x\in\Zp$ and publishes
$X=xG_2$. For explicit messages $m_0,\ldots,m_{\ell-1}$, nullifier seed
$m_{\rm null}$, address scalar $m_{\rm Addr}$, and hidden blinding scalar
$m_\gamma$, define
\begin{align}
 C_J &= G_1+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}m_iH_i
            +m_{\rm null}H_{\rm null}
            +m_{\rm Addr}H_{\rm Addr},                 \label{eq:cj}\\
 C   &= C_J+m_\gamma H_\gamma.                         \label{eq:c}
\end{align}
The partially blind signing phase returns $\sigma=(A,e_s)$, where
\begin{equation}
 A=(x+e_s)^{-1}C.                                      \label{eq:bbs}
\end{equation}
We write the signature scalar as $e_s$ to distinguish it from the pairing.

To prove possession, the user selects randomizers $r,\alpha,\beta,\delta$
and computes
\begin{align}
 \bar A &= rA,                                         \label{eq:abar}\\
 \bar B &= rC-re_sA=rC-e_s\bar A=x\bar A,             \label{eq:bbar}\\
 U      &= \alpha C_J+\beta\bar A+\delta H_\gamma.    \label{eq:u}
\end{align}
The Fiat--Shamir challenge and responses are
\begin{align}
 c &= H(\mathit{ctx}\parallel\vec m_J\parallel
          \bar A\parallel\bar B\parallel U),          \label{eq:challenge}\\
 s &= \alpha+rc,\qquad
 t=\beta-e_sc,\qquad
 u_\gamma=\delta+rm_\gamma c.                         \label{eq:responses}
\end{align}
The proof is
$\pi=(\bar A,\bar B,U,s,t,u_\gamma)$.

The pinned Solidity verifier serializes $\mathit{ctx}$, every disclosed
scalar as a fixed-width 32-byte word, and the affine $x,y$ coordinates of
$\bar A,\bar B,U$ using \texttt{abi.encodePacked}. It reduces Keccak-256
modulo the BN254 scalar-field order. This byte-level detail does not help the
attack: its equations hold for every resulting $c$.

\subsection{Ordinary verification}

After reconstructing $C_J$, the paper accepts exactly when
\begin{align}
 e(\bar A,X) &= e(\bar B,G_2),                         \label{eq:pairing}\\
 U+c\bar B   &= sC_J+t\bar A+u_\gamma H_\gamma.       \label{eq:linear}
\end{align}
The source implements Equation~\eqref{eq:pairing} as one two-pair BN254
precompile call and Equation~\eqref{eq:linear} with the ordinary addition and
scalar-multiplication precompiles. It exposes an \texttt{isInfinity}
function that recognizes $(0,0)$, and \texttt{negate} preserves that value,
but \texttt{verifyProof} never rejects an identity input.

The source profile reconstructs $C_J$ from the five disclosed words and omits
the paper's address term. The paper-complete experiment restores that term.
Because the attack multiplies all of $C_J$ by $s=0$, the distinction has no
effect on acceptance and is not part of the attack mechanism.

\subsection{Exact Sys-EUF-CMA endpoint}

Definition~1 of the target paper gives the following system game. The
challenger runs $\Init(1^\lambda)$ and gives the adversary $X$ and the public
parameters. The adversary may query an interactive blind issuance oracle
$\mathcal O_{\rm Sign}$ with a commitment $C_2^{(i)}$ and an explicit
attribute vector
\[
 \vec m_{\rm exp}^{(i)}=(m_0^{(i)},\ldots,m_{\ell-1}^{(i)}).
\]
Let $Q$ be the set of explicit vectors successfully signed. The adversary
outputs
\[
 (\pi^\star,\vec m_{\rm exp}^\star,m_{\rm null}^\star,
  m_{\rm Addr}^\star,M^\star)
\]
and wins when
\begin{equation}
 \Verify(pp,X,\pi^\star,M^\star,m_{\rm null}^\star,
         m_{\rm Addr}^\star)=1
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \vec m_{\rm exp}^\star\notin Q.                     \label{eq:game}
\end{equation}
Thus freshness is explicit-vector freshness. The paper claims security under
q-SDH and simulation extractability of the proof system. The attack below
violates the proof-system premise before either hardness assumption is
relevant.

\section{Zero-query identity-proof forgery}

Let $\OO$ denote the identity of $\GOne$, and $1_T$ the identity of $\GT$.
The attack is a single fixed proof:
\begin{equation}
 \pi_0=(\bar A,\bar B,U,s,t,u_\gamma)
      =(\OO,\OO,\OO,0,0,0).                           \label{eq:zero-proof}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:forge}
For every CUBE public key, base set, disclosed attribute vector, address,
nullifier, context, and Fiat--Shamir challenge, $\pi_0$ satisfies the ordinary
verification equations. Consequently, an adversary wins the paper's
Sys-EUF-CMA game with zero signing-oracle queries and probability one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bilinearity gives $e(\OO,Q)=1_T$ for every $Q\in\GTwo$. Therefore
\[
 e(\bar A,X)=e(\OO,X)=1_T=e(\OO,G_2)=e(\bar B,G_2),
\]
so Equation~\eqref{eq:pairing} holds. The verifier computes some challenge
$c$ from the supplied statement and zero points. Its value is immaterial:
\[
 U+c\bar B=\OO+c\OO=\OO,
\]
while
\[
 sC_J+t\bar A+u_\gamma H_\gamma
 =0C_J+0\OO+0H_\gamma=\OO.
\]
Thus Equation~\eqref{eq:linear} also holds.

The adversary makes no query to $\mathcal O_{\rm Sign}$, hence $Q=\varnothing$.
It chooses any explicit attribute vector $\vec m_{\rm exp}^\star$ and outputs
$\pi_0$ with any syntactically valid public payload. Ordinary verification
returns one, and
$\vec m_{\rm exp}^\star\notin\varnothing$. Both conditions in
Equation~\eqref{eq:game} hold. No probabilistic event remains.
\end{proof}

The adversary's cryptographic work is constant: encode three identity points
and three zero scalars. In the Solidity ABI the three affine points occupy six
words and the responses occupy three more, so the complete proof body is nine
zero words. The attack performs no pairing, scalar multiplication, search,
hash inversion, or signing operation. The verifier still computes its normal
challenge and full equations.

\paragraph{Fresh signing consequence.}
The explicit vector is the security game's signed message. With $Q$ empty,
every chosen vector is fresh. At the application layer, the attacker selects
the issuer's advertised constraints and an unused nullifier/address payload.
The public nullifier-precommit step in the reference wrapper is a state
prerequisite, not issuance authorization and not a signature. The canonical
simulation in Section~\ref{sec:deployed} confirms that the ordinary mint path
reaches and accepts the zero proof once that prerequisite exists. We make no
claim that this audit broadcast such a transaction.

\paragraph{Why this is a design attack.}
The identity is a valid element of the prime-order group and is expressly
handled by the source. The paper itself prints Equations~\eqref{eq:pairing}
and~\eqref{eq:linear} without a nonidentity condition. The proof remains valid
with nonidentity public keys and bases and with every paper-level message term
present. A faithful, otherwise competent implementation of the stated
predicate therefore accepts it.

separation from the primary run.

\section{Security consequence and proof failure}

The target paper's unforgeability theorem reduces a successful proof to a BBS
signature forgery by invoking simulation extractability or proof of knowledge.
Theorem~\ref{thm:forge} exhibits an accepting transcript that contains no BBS
signature witness at all. Therefore the stated proof system does not satisfy
the knowledge premise on the verifier's accepted domain. Rewinding the fixed
identity commitments under another challenge yields another all-zero accepting
transcript; subtracting responses reveals only zeros, not a witness satisfying
the claimed relation.

The result does not challenge q-SDH or the unforgeability of a correctly
validated BBS signature or proof system. It shows that CUBE's acceptance
predicate is broader than the relation assumed by the reduction. The normal
hardness assumptions never become active on the identity branch.

The immediate impact is an efficient credential-proof forgery for arbitrary
explicit attributes under the paper's game. It does not recover the signer's
secret key or an equivalent signing key. Nor does it evaluate privacy,
blindness, unlinkability, or traceability after a valid issuance; the violated
property is system unforgeability and its ordinary verification/minting
endpoint.

\section{Prior art and final attack-history result}

The identity-element mechanism and its validation rule are not new. The 23
October 2022 CFRG BBS draft requires each of its three serialized proof points
to decode to a valid G1 point different from the identity~\cite{bbsdraft01}.
The current draft retains identity rejection~\cite{bbsdraft13}, and a 2025
CFRG discussion expressly notes nonidentity validation for BBS proof

\section{Repair directions}

At minimum, verification must reject the group identity for every proof point
whose protocol role requires a nonidentity value, before pairing or linear
checks. In this construction that means validating $\bar A$, $\bar B$, and
$U$ as canonical, on-curve, correct-subgroup, nonidentity G1 elements. The
prover should sample the signature randomizer from $\Zp^*$ and abort or
resample on any forbidden identity. The decoder and smart contract should
apply the same normative rule. Checking whether the Fiat--Shamir challenge is
zero does not help because Equation~\eqref{eq:zero-proof} works for every
challenge.

These checks are necessary to remove the demonstrated branch, but this audit
does not claim they constitute a complete proof repair. A revision should
restate the exact NP relation and accepted domain, prove special soundness or
simulation extractability for that domain, and test the implementation against
all-identity proofs and single-component mutations. The unforgeability
reduction must extract a genuine BBS witness from every accepted proof rather
than assume that fact.

The source and paper should also be reconciled so that the address term
$m_{\rm Addr}H_{\rm Addr}$ is bound consistently. That separate issue is not
needed for this attack and adding the term alone does not block it. Any repair
should be tested in both the complete paper profile and the deployed wrapper,
including nullifier state transitions and negative controls. We have not
implemented or validated a patched CUBE design.

\section{Limitations}

The evidence and claim are bounded as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the attack targets the initial/current ePrint 2026/920 object and the
        pinned author source commit named above;
  \item the fresh-message consequence is the explicit-attribute-vector
        freshness defined by the paper's Sys-EUF-CMA game;
  \item EXP-0001 and EXP-0005 use synthetic valid BN254 keys, bases, and
        scalars in an exact-equation conformance harness; they do not execute
        an EVM or deployed bytecode;
  \item EXP-0004 establishes read-only simulated behavior at author-listed
        Sepolia addresses; it sent no transaction, used no private key, and
        does not claim a broadcast forged mint;
  \item no independent compilation established a source-to-runtime-bytecode
        match for those addresses;
  \item the source's missing address term is a separate conformance issue and
        is not used as the attack mechanism;
  \item the deterministic executions establish concrete full-parameter
        instances rather than a random-trial success frequency; universality
        follows from the algebraic proof;
  \item the audit claims fresh-message proof forgery, not secret-key or
        equivalent-key recovery;
  \item the history conclusion is limited to public material accessible and
        indexed by the search cutoff; and
  \item independent human verification was not performed and is optional,
        nonblocking provenance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

CUBE's verifier accepts three proof points without excluding the group
identity. At that point, both the pairing relation and the Fiat--Shamir linear
relation become identity equalities, regardless of the challenge, public key,
messages, context, or bases. A fixed nine-word zero proof therefore wins the
paper's Sys-EUF-CMA game with no signing query and no witness.

Actual BN254 pairings, the paper-complete address-bound profile, the pinned
Solidity transcript, discriminating controls, a canonical read-only deployed
simulation, hostile review, and fresh-context independent reproduction all
confirm the same result. The repair principle is established generic BBS
prior art, and no technique novelty is claimed. The CUBE-specific conclusion
is nevertheless direct: its ordinary full-parameter verifier admits an
efficient fresh-message credential forgery.

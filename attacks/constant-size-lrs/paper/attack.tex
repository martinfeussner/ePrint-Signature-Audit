\begin{abstract}
We analyze revision~2 of \emph{Efficient Constant-Size Linkable Ring
Signatures for Ad-Hoc Rings via Pairing-Based Set Membership Arguments},
IACR ePrint 2025/1174. Here ``zero-query'' means zero signing-oracle queries;
the construction still makes the ordinary Fiat--Shamir random-oracle
queries. The optimized set-membership argument derives its base challenges
from the message tag and commitments but omits the ordered ring
$\Phi=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_n)$. We exploit that omission at the authors'
full BN254 evaluation profile, $n=m=1024$.

Before the aggregate challenge, an attacker pairs 508 honest KGO public-key
hashes into 254 unordered pairs and fixes one coefficient per pair as
$2^j/(a_j-c_j)$. After the challenge, orienting pair $j$ changes the weighted
membership residue by exactly $-2^j$. Since the BN254 scalar modulus is less
than $2^{254}$, the 254 orientations span every required field residue. The
attacker thereby cancels the sole coefficient whose SRS power is unavailable,
constructs the SMA proof from public powers, and computes the later
ring-bound KZG opening honestly. A separately generated valid secret outside
the ring supplies honest witnesses for ccGro16 and CPlink. The final ring is a
permutation of 1,024 distinct honest KGO outputs and does not contain the
attacker's key.

A prospectively sealed standard-library implementation executed the exact
SMA Equation~15 and KZG Equation~17 polynomial identities at the full paper
profile. The deterministic Python paper model, not an end-to-end author
implementation, completed in 63.85 seconds using 24,576~KiB peak RSS on one
worker; a hostile \texttt{python -O} replay completed in 64.27 seconds using
28,220~KiB. Both emitted byte-identical evidence. A separate validator checked
all 1,024 BabyJubJub curve equations and MiMC91 hashes plus the pair-swap
arithmetic. Fixed-order, one-pair-flip, and changed-message controls rejected.
The experiment does not emit concrete
ccGro16 or CPlink proofs: it composes their honest-witness relations by
correctness. No complete author LRS verifier, outer codec, or known-answer
test was located in the published artifact, so we make no byte-level
author-verifier claim. No secret-key recovery is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Xie, Tu, Au, Fang, Wang, and Jiang propose a constant-size scoped linkable
ring signature for ad-hoc rings~\cite{lrs}. Its ordinary verification is a
conjunction of three proof systems. An optimized pairing-based set-membership
argument (SMA) is intended to show that a committed public-key hash belongs to
the ring; ccGro16 shows correct public-key and link-tag computation; and
CPlink ties the committed values together. The paper's evaluated BN254
profile uses BabyJubJub public keys, a two-input 91-round MiMC7 hash, and rings
of up to 1,024 members. The paper describes this row as 128-bit and reports a
687-byte algebraic signature. We use ``authors' claimed 128-bit row'' without
independently endorsing that contemporary security estimate.

The attack is a statement-binding failure in the optimized SMA transcript.
The aggregation challenge is fixed before the ordered ring is hashed. Its
soundness analysis treats every missing-degree relation error as fixed at
that time, but the membership error still depends on the ring. An adversary
can therefore retain 254 independent pair orientations until after seeing
the challenge and use them as a complete binary basis for the BN254 scalar
field. This is not a collision search, subset-sum search, powers-of-tau
attack, or malformed-key attack.

\paragraph{Contribution.}
We derive the exact unavailable-degree coefficient in Equation~15, give a
deterministic full-profile pair-orientation algorithm, prove that it yields a
false-membership signature under the paper semantics, connect the result to
the authors' unforgeability game, and report a prospectively sealed
full-parameter execution with independently checked controls. We do not
claim novelty for the general requirement that Fiat--Shamir challenges bind
their complete statement~\cite{fiatshamir}, nor for polynomial commitments
or missing-power aggregation~\cite{kzg}. The contribution is the concrete
attack on this scheme and profile.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The positive execution is exact for the SMA Equation~15 and KZG Equation~17
algebra. The other two conjuncts use ordinary honest witnesses and are
composed by completeness; no concrete ccGro16 or CPlink proof bytes are
emitted. The published source is a partial benchmark and reference artifact,
not a complete external LRS verifier. Our attack is therefore a paper-level
design result, not a wire-format or author-binary conformance claim.

\section{The optimized SMA relation}

We work in exponents over $\F$. Let $X$ be a formal variable representing
the powers-of-tau point and let the outside attacker key hash be
$\phi_\star$. For commitment randomness $r,r_b,r_s$, define
\begin{align}
A(X)&=r+\phi_\star X,\label{eq:A}\\
B(X)&=r_b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_iX^i,\label{eq:B}\\
S(X)&=r_s+\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_i b_iX^{n+1-i}.\label{eq:S}
\end{align}
The attacker forms $S$ in the honest way from $s_i b_i$, even though the
chosen $b_i$ are not bits.

For $m=n$, the public polynomials entering Equation~15 are
\begin{align}
P_h(X)&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
 \bigl[\delta_o+(\delta_{eq}t_i-\delta_b)s_i
       +\delta_\phi\phi_i\bigr]X^{n+1-i},\label{eq:ph}\\
P_u(X)&=\delta_\phi X^n-\delta_{d2}X^{n-1}
 -\sum_{i=2}^{n}\delta_{d1}u_iX^{n+1-i},\label{eq:pu}\\
P_t(X)&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{eq}t_iX^i.\label{eq:pt}
\end{align}
The required G1 proof exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
F(X)=B(X)\bigl[\delta_bS(X)+P_h(X)\bigr]
     -A(X)P_u(X)-S(X)P_t(X)-\delta_oX^{n+1}.
\end{equation}
Its degree is at most $2n$. The public G1 SRS contains every needed power in
that range except $X^{n+1}$. Consequently a prover using only the public SRS
can form the proof exactly when the coefficient at degree $n+1$ is zero.

\subsection{The sole unavailable coefficient}

Collecting degree $n+1$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:F} gives
\begin{align}
[X^{n+1}]F
={}&\delta_b\sum_i s_i b_i^2
 +\sum_i b_i\bigl[\delta_o+(\delta_{eq}t_i-\delta_b)s_i
                         +\delta_\phi\phi_i\bigr]\notag\\
&-\delta_{eq}\sum_i s_i b_i t_i
 -\delta_\phi\phi_\star-\delta_o.\label{eq:expanded}
\end{align}
The two $\delta_{eq}$ terms cancel exactly because $S$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:S} is formed honestly. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:missing}
E=[X^{n+1}]F
=\delta_b\sum_i s_i b_i(b_i-1)
 +\delta_o\left(\sum_i b_i-1\right)
 +\delta_\phi\left(\sum_i b_i\phi_i-\phi_\star\right).
\end{equation}
The three summands are respectively the binary, one-hot, and membership
errors aggregated by the verifier.

\subsection{Where statement binding is lost}

The base oracles $H_s$, $H_t$, $H_u$, and $H_{agg}$ absorb the message tag
and the relevant commitments, but not the ordered ring $\Phi$. The paper's
message-binding remark adds the message tag to these inputs and does not add
the ring. Therefore $A$, $B$, $S$, every $s_i$, and all six aggregation
coefficients can be fixed while pair orientations inside $\Phi$ remain open.

The outsourced KZG point is sampled later. Its transcript includes the final
ring and the commitments to $P_h$, $P_u$, and $P_t$. This later binding is
useful for a control, but it cannot retroactively make the membership error in
Equation~\eqref{eq:missing} fixed before $H_{agg}$.

\section{Adaptive ring-permutation attack}

The adversary generates a valid key $(sk_\star,\phi_\star)$ and obtains a
pool of 1,024 distinct honest KGO public-key hashes not containing
$\phi_\star$. Choose 254 disjoint unordered pairs
\[
(a_j,c_j),\qquad 0\leq j<254,
\]
using 508 pool entries. The other 516 entries occupy zero-coefficient
positions. Initially place $a_j$ in the first position of pair $j$ and $c_j$
in the second. Before querying the aggregate oracle, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bpair}
b_{2j+1}=\frac{2^j}{a_j-c_j},\qquad b_{2j+2}=0.
\end{equation}
All denominators are public and nonzero; the negligible event of equal hashes
inside a selected pair is detectable and handled by replacing that pair with
another honest KGO output.

Swapping the two entries of pair $j$ changes its contribution to the weighted
membership sum by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swap}
\frac{2^j c_j}{a_j-c_j}-\frac{2^j a_j}{a_j-c_j}=-2^j.
\end{equation}
After deriving $\delta$, define the already fixed errors
\begin{equation}
E_b=\sum_i s_i b_i(b_i-1),\qquad E_o=\sum_i b_i-1,
\end{equation}
and, provided $\delta_\phi\neq0$, the target weighted value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
W=\phi_\star-\frac{\delta_bE_b+\delta_oE_o}{\delta_\phi}.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
B_0=\sum_{j=0}^{253}\frac{2^j a_j}{a_j-c_j},
\qquad D=(B_0-W)\bmod q,\qquad 0\leq D<q.
\end{equation}
By Equation~\eqref{eq:q}, $D$ has a 254-bit expansion
\[
D=\sum_{j=0}^{253}\epsilon_j2^j,qquad\epsilon_j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Swap pair $j$ exactly when $\epsilon_j=1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:swap} then
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
\sum_i b_i\phi_i=B_0-\sum_j\epsilon_j2^j=B_0-D=W\pmod q.
\end{equation}
Substitution of Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted} into
Equation~\eqref{eq:missing} yields $E=0$ exactly.

\subsection{Complete attack chronology}

The following order respects every hash dependency in revision~2.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Generate the outside key, fix the fresh message and scope, choose
        $r$, and form the commitment represented by $A(X)$.
  \item Fix the unordered KGO pair pool, every $b_i$, and $r_b$, then form
        the commitment represented by $B(X)$. Only pair orientation remains
        open.
  \item Query $H_s$, choose $r_s$, form the honest $S(X)$, and query
        $H_t$, the $H_u$ oracles, and $H_{agg}$.
  \item Compute $D$ and orient all 254 pairs from its bits. This changes no
        input to a base oracle.
  \item Expand Equation~\eqref{eq:F}. Because its sole unavailable
        coefficient is zero, form the SMA proof from public SRS powers.
  \item Compute $P_h,P_u,P_t$ from the final ring and produce the later KZG
        commitments and quotient opening honestly for that ring.
  \item Use $sk_\star$ and the known commitment randomness with the honest
        ccGro16 and CPlink provers. These components are not forged.
\end{enumerate}
If $\delta_\phi=0$, which occurs with probability $1/q$ in the random-oracle
model, discard the transcript and begin another. The retained deterministic
row has $\delta_\phi\neq0$.

\section{Correctness and signing consequence}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:forge}
Under correctness of ccGro16 and CPlink, the algorithm above produces a
paper-level revision-2 LRS signature on an attacker-chosen fresh message for a
ring of 1,024 honest KGO outputs, although the attacker's public-key hash is
not in the ring. It makes no signing-oracle query and succeeds except for the
$1/q$ event $\delta_\phi=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every ring entry is an unchanged honest KGO output; the algorithm only
permutes entries inside fixed pairs. Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted} makes the
coefficient in Equation~\eqref{eq:missing} zero, so every nonzero coefficient
of $F$ is supported by a published SRS power and Equation~15 holds. The later
KZG commitments and opening are computed from the final ring, so Equation~17
holds by polynomial-commitment correctness. The outside valid secret satisfies
the ccGro16 public-key/link-tag relation, and the same committed
$\phi_\star$ with known randomness satisfies the CPlink common-opening
relation. Correctness supplies accepting proofs for those honest statements.
Their conjunction therefore satisfies the paper verifier. The ring pool was
chosen disjoint from $\phi_\star$, so the accepted membership statement is
false. The message was fixed without a signing-oracle query and is fresh.
\end{proof}

This meets the paper's unforgeability-game boundary: the adversary chooses an
ad-hoc ring from honest KGO outputs, corrupts no ring key, requests no
signature, and outputs an accepted signature under a ring for which it knows
no signing key. Owning a valid key outside that ring does not authorize the
signature. Algebraically, the same construction can also produce two accepted
signatures at one scope from two outside secrets with distinct link tags,
yielding the paper's scoped-linkability consequence. That two-signature
extension was not separately executed; the experiment executed one fresh
false-membership forgery, which is the primary classification.

\section{Reproduction and results}

The experiment was prospectively sealed before execution. Its seed, message,
scope, KGO derivation, pair allocation, exceptional-event rule, and controls
were fixed. The implementation uses Python's standard library. It recomputes
all 1,024 KGO outputs using the pinned author-source MiMC constants and models
SMA/KZG group elements as formal public-SRS coefficient vectors. Exact
coefficient identities are stronger than equality at one hidden setup point
for the positive row, while making the missing power explicit.

The host reported an AMD EPYC Processor under KVM with 64 virtual CPUs
available. Each recorded replay was deliberately fixed to one Python worker
and one thread; the available virtual-CPU count is not a worker count.

An independent execution ran the frozen program normally and again under
\texttt{python -O}; the second run ensures success does not depend on removed
assertions. The full replay regenerated all 1,024 public points from the
prospectively fixed scalar stream and recomputed the complete transcript. A
separately written validator does not import the attack program. It checks the
evidence schema, every public position, all BabyJubJub curve equations, all

residue bits, attacker-key absence, and positive/control predicate records.
By itself, that validator does not derive the public points from committed KGO

\subsection{Exact implementation boundary}

The program executes the SMA Equation~15 polynomial identity and the KZG
Equation~17 quotient identity at the full paper profile. It checks the exact
ccGro16 witness relation and CPlink common opening used by the attacker, but
does not instantiate those relations as concrete group proof objects. Its
internal \texttt{ordinary\_accept} flag is therefore a composition
certificate under completeness, not an observation from a complete author
LRS verifier.

The author repository contains a partial benchmark/reference implementation.
No complete external KeyGen/Sign/Verify/Link API, complete ordinary LRS
verifier, outer 687-byte codec, or author known-answer test was located in the
published artifact. Consequently, no complete author-verifier byte string was
available against which to make a stronger claim. The paper semantics remain
the normative target.

\section{Limitations and repair direction}

\paragraph{Ring-order lifecycle.}
The attack needs freedom to choose the final order of an otherwise fixed
multiset after the base commitments. That freedom is present in the paper's
ad-hoc-ring API and security game. An application that externally fixes,
canonically orders, and authenticates the ring before all proof commitments
could block this exact route; revision~2 does not specify such a lifecycle.

\paragraph{Paper/source scalar mismatch.}
The paper samples $sk$ from the full BN254 scalar field, while the available
cc circuit decomposes 253 bits. The deterministic outside secret used in the
experiment is below $2^{253}$ and so lies in the circuit's domain. This does
not resolve the broader mismatch and is another reason not to claim complete
source conformance.

\paragraph{Repair.}
Every Fiat--Shamir oracle that determines the aggregate SMA relation should
bind the complete ordered ring statement before any proof value can be
adapted. If a deployment instead relies on canonical ordering, the canonical
encoding and its authentication must be normative and included in the
transcript. Either change requires a new security proof, parameter review,
codec definition, and interoperability tests. We do not validate a drop-in
patch.

\section{Attack history and related technique}

No prior public substantive attack against ePrint 2025/1174 revision 2 was
located in the searches performed through 2026-10-23. Those searches covered the exact title,
identifier, DOI, aliases, authors, repository history, citations, forums,
talks, related ring-signature work, and attack terminology. This is a dated,
bounded negative result; it cannot exclude private, deleted, unpublished, or
unindexed work. Work on other ring signatures does not establish an attack on
this target.

The general lesson is classical: a Fiat--Shamir challenge must bind the full
statement whose errors it aggregates~\cite{fiatshamir}. Likewise, KZG
commitments and missing-power techniques are established tools~\cite{kzg}.
We make no claim that adaptive statement selection, binary residue control,
or polynomial commitment algebra is new in general. The result identifies
their concrete interaction with the revision-2 Constant-Size LRS transcript.

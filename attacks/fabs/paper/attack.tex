\begin{abstract}
We analyze the key-policy attribute-based signature (KP-ABS) construction in
Figure~1 of \emph{FABS: Fast Attribute-Based Signatures}, IACR ePrint
2026/022. Its signature contains group elements $(A,B,C)$ and a
Fiat--Shamir proof for
$Y=X^\beta$ and
$B=g_1^k\prod_i H_1(\pi(i))^{\delta_i}$, where
$Y=e(A,g_2)/e(B,C)$. The verifier does not prove that $(A,B,C)$ came from an
issued credential, does not bind the public witnesses to MSP reconstruction
coefficients, and does not require them to be nonzero.

For any public key, legal attribute set, and attacker-chosen fresh message,
set $A=B=g_1$ and $C=g_2$. Then $Y=1$. Choose a public scalar $\rho$, query
\[
 c=H_0(g_1,g_1,g_2,1,1,g_1^\rho,\mathit{msg}),
\]
and return $s_\alpha=0$, $s_k=\rho-c$, and $s_i=0$ for every attribute.
The ordinary verifier reconstructs exactly the tuple queried to $H_0$ and
accepts. All three transmitted group elements are nonidentity. The adversary
makes one $H_0$ query, no $H_1$ query, no key-generation query, and no signing
query; as a mathematical algorithm it may use the known target-group identity
without computing a pairing. An all-identity presentation is still simpler, while a
strengthened presentation makes $A,B,C,Z,W$ nonidentity and every scalar
attribute response nonzero using only public data.

A sealed standard-library typed-group model accepted every attack at legal
1-, 3-, and 100-attribute counts, plus algebraic 0-attribute edge checks,
three honest controls, and four separately formed
fresh-message forgeries; eight transcript-perturbation controls rejected. The
full run took 0.11 seconds and 18,432 KiB peak resident memory on one worker.
A hostile reviewer re-derived the paper equations and returned PASS. The
algebra holds in any prime-order Type-III pairing group. The model uses a
common explicit BN254 scalar order, but the exact Charm/PBC backend and wire
encoding used by the authors remain unresolved, so no byte-conformance claim
is made. A prospectively sealed fresh-context implementation using actual
Arkworks BN254 pairing operations reproduced both retained 100-attribute
attack forms in five of five fixed setup checks. In every run, honest 1-, 3-, and
100-attribute controls accepted and eight single-change negative controls
rejected. The sealed run used zero key-generation and signing queries, took
3.77 seconds wall time and 31,744 KiB peak resident memory, and used one
program thread and one Cargo build job.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Chen et al. present FABS, a pair of pairing-based attribute-based signature
schemes supporting monotone span program (MSP) policies, large universes,
arbitrary attributes, and adaptive security~\cite{fabs}. The KP-ABS
construction embeds a policy in the signing key and associates each signature
with a public set of attributes. The paper claims adaptive EUF-CMA security in
the random-oracle model under the computational bilinear Diffie--Hellman
assumption. Its implementation evaluation uses the BN254 curve and reaches
100 attributes.

The final signature proof verifies a smaller relation than the security
argument needs. The printed Schnorr statement~\cite{schnorr} allows the witness
$(\beta,k,\{\delta_i\})=(0,1,\{0\})$, which is public and satisfies the
relation when $A=B=g_1$ and $C=g_2$. The Fiat--Shamir
transform~\cite{fiatshamir} therefore yields responses after one ordinary
$H_0$ query. No issued credential, signing
transcript, master secret, pairing computation, collision, preimage, or
exceptional setup is required.

\paragraph{Contribution and originality boundary.}
We give the exact all-identity, nonidentity, and strengthened nonidentity
forgeries; connect them to the paper's EUF-CMA game and 100-attribute workload;
identify the failed premise in the proof; report primary and hostile-review
evidence; and provide a self-contained public reproducer. We make no claim
that public-witness Schnorr transcripts, identity-element validation, or the
Fiat--Shamir reasoning is novel. The contribution is their concrete
application to the literal FABS Figure~1 verifier.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The result breaks the KP-ABS construction's unforgeability. It does not claim
an attack on the separate SP-ABS construction or on anonymity. It is a
construction-level paper result and does not depend on the author source's
separate MSP-coefficient defect.

\section{FABS KP-ABS and its claimed security}

\subsection{Setup and signing relation}

Let $p$ be prime and let $\gone$, $\gtwo$, and $\gthree$ be cyclic groups of
order $p$, with generators $g_1\in\gone$, $g_2\in\gtwo$, and asymmetric
pairing $e:\gone\times\gtwo\rightarrow\gthree$. Setup samples
$\alpha\leftarrow\Zp^*$ and publishes
\[
 X=e(g_1,g_2)^\alpha.
\]
The random oracles are $H_0:\{0,1\}^*\rightarrow\Zp$ and
$H_1:\{0,1\}^*\rightarrow\gone$.

A KP signing key is associated with an MSP $(M,\pi)$. For a satisfying set of
rows $I$, the signer finds coefficients $\gamma_i$ such that
$\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_i M_i=(1,0,\ldots,0)$. Figure~1 samples
$k,t,r_\alpha,r_k,\{r_i\}\leftarrow\Zp$ and creates group elements
$A\in\gone$, $B\in\gone$, $C\in\gtwo$ that satisfy
\[
 Y=\frac{e(A,g_2)}{e(B,C)}=X^{kt},
 \qquad
 B=g_1^k\prod_{i\in I}H_1(\pi(i))^{\gamma_i k}.
\]
The Fiat--Shamir commitments and challenge are
\[
 Z=X^{r_\alpha},\qquad
 W=g_1^{r_k}\prod_{i\in I}H_1(\pi(i))^{r_i},
\]
\[
 c=H_0(A,B,C,Y,Z,W,\mathit{msg}),
\]
with responses
\[
 s_\alpha=r_\alpha-ktc,\quad
 s_k=r_k-kc,\quad
 s_i=r_i-\gamma_i kc.
\]

\subsection{Literal verification}

For signature
$\sigma=(A,B,C,c,s_\alpha,s_k,\{s_i\}_{i\in I})$, Figure~1 computes
\begin{align*}
Y'&=\frac{e(A,g_2)}{e(B,C)},\\
Z'&=X^{s_\alpha}(Y')^c,\\
W'&=g_1^{s_k}\prod_{i\in I}H_1(\pi(i))^{s_i}B^c,\\
c'&=H_0(A,B,C,Y',Z',W',\mathit{msg}),
\end{align*}
and accepts exactly when $c'=c$.

Equivalently, the printed signature proof establishes knowledge of
$(\beta,k,\{\delta_i\})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:printed-relation}
Y=X^\beta
\quad\text{and}\quad
B=g_1^k\prod_{i\in I}H_1(\pi(i))^{\delta_i}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:printed-relation} does not state that $(A,B,C)$ came from a
credential, that the credential's policy accepts the attribute set, or that
$\beta=kt$ and $\delta_i=\gamma_i k$ for shared values.

\subsection{EUF-CMA endpoint}

In the paper's KP-ABS game, a forgery is
$(\mathit{msg}^*,y^*,\sigma^*)$, where $y^*$ is the public attribute set. It
must pass ordinary verification. The adversary must not have requested a key
whose policy accepts $y^*$ and must not have made a forbidden signing query
for $y^*$ or under a policy accepting $y^*$. The campaign also requires that
$\mathit{msg}^*$ never appeared in a signing query. An adversary making zero
key and signing queries satisfies all these restrictions vacuously.

\section{Universal fresh-message forgery}

\subsection{Numbered attack algorithm}

For a public key, a legal public attribute set indexed by $I$, and an
attacker-chosen fresh message, the minimal nonidentity adversary proceeds as
follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Choose a public nonzero scalar $\rho$ and set
        $A=B=g_1$, $C=g_2$, $Y=\onethree$, $Z=\onethree$, and
        $W=g_1^\rho$.
  \item Make the sole challenge-oracle query
        $c=H_0(A,B,C,Y,Z,W,\mathit{msg})$.
  \item Set $s_\alpha=0$, $s_k=\rho-c$, and $s_i=0$ for every $i\in I$.
  \item Return $(A,B,C,c,s_\alpha,s_k,\{s_i\}_{i\in I})$.
\end{enumerate}
This algorithm makes no key-generation or signing query. The cancellation
$Y=1$ is known algebraically, so the mathematical adversary need not compute a
pairing.

\subsection{All-identity form}

For any chosen message and attribute set, let
\[
A=\oneone,\quad B=\oneone,\quad C=\onetwo,
\quad s_\alpha=s_k=s_i=0.
\]
The verifier obtains $Y'=\onethree$, $Z'=\onethree$, and $W'=\oneone$.
Query
\[
c=H_0(\oneone,\oneone,\onetwo,\onethree,\onethree,
      \oneone,\mathit{msg})
\]
and insert the result into the signature. Verification repeats the same tuple
for every returned value $c$ and accepts deterministically.

These are valid typed group elements and scalars. Figure~1 includes no
identity rejection. It deliberately writes $\Zp$ rather than $\Zp^*$ for the
signer's $k,t,r_\alpha,r_k,r_i$, so a nonzero convention cannot be inferred
from the sampling syntax.

\subsection{Nonidentity transmitted components}

Identity rejection in the three transmitted group slots does not stop the
attack.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonidentity}
For every FABS KP-ABS public key, every legal public attribute set, and every
chosen message, the literal Figure~1 verifier accepts a signature formed with
one $H_0$ query, no $H_1$ query, no pairing, no key-generation query, no
signing query, and nonidentity $A$, $B$, and $C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a public scalar $\rho\in\Zp$ and set
\[
A=g_1,\qquad B=g_1,\qquad C=g_2.
\]
Then
\[
Y'=\frac{e(g_1,g_2)}{e(g_1,g_2)}=\onethree.
\]
Fix the prechallenge commitments $Z=\onethree$ and $W=g_1^\rho$, and query
\[
c=H_0(g_1,g_1,g_2,\onethree,\onethree,g_1^\rho,
      \mathit{msg}).
\]
Return
\[
s_\alpha=0,\qquad s_k=\rho-c,\qquad s_i=0
\quad\text{for every }i.
\]
The verifier reconstructs
\[
Z'=X^0(\onethree)^c=\onethree
\]
and
\[
W'=g_1^{\rho-c}\prod_i H_1(\pi(i))^0(g_1)^c=g_1^\rho.
\]
It therefore queries $H_0$ on exactly the prechallenge tuple and obtains
$c'=c$. The witnesses $(\beta,k,\{\delta_i\})=(0,1,\{0\})$ satisfy
Equation~\eqref{eq:printed-relation}. Every operation used by the forger is
public, and $g_1,g_1,g_2$ are nonidentity by definition.
\end{proof}

There is no Fiat--Shamir fixed point in this construction. The group tuple
given to $H_0$ is completely fixed before the challenge is requested;
$s_k=\rho-c$ merely makes verification reconstruct the preselected $W$.

\subsection{Strengthened nonidentity commitments and responses}

The zero commitments and attribute responses are also unnecessary. Keep
$A=B=g_1$, $C=g_2$ and public witnesses
$(\beta,k,\delta_i)=(0,1,0)$. Choose $r_\alpha=1$, public nonzero values
$r_i$, and an $r_k$ for which
\[
W=g_1^{r_k}\prod_i H_1(\pi(i))^{r_i}\ne\oneone.
\]
At most one value of $r_k$ is excluded, so it can be selected publicly without
a secret. Set $Z=X$, query
\[
c=H_0(g_1,g_1,g_2,\onethree,X,W,\mathit{msg}),
\]
and return
\[
s_\alpha=1,\qquad s_k=r_k-c,\qquad s_i=r_i.
\]
The verifier reconstructs $Z'=X$ and the selected $W$. Since setup chooses
$\alpha\in\Zp^*$, $X$ is nonidentity. Thus $A$, $B$, $C$, $Z'$, and $W'$ can
all be nonidentity while every scalar attribute response is nonzero; only the
derived cancellation $Y'=\onethree$ remains.

\section{Full-size instance, complexity, and freshness}

The paper evaluates KP-ABS with policy and attribute sizes through 100. When
both sizes agree, it forms an AND gate over all attributes. The reproducer
uses the standard telescoping 100-row MSP: its first row is
$(1,1,0,\ldots)$, intermediate rows are
$(0,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots)$, and its last row ends in $-1$. The all-one
reconstruction coefficients sum the rows to $(1,0,\ldots,0)$. This is a legal
100-distinct-attribute all-AND workload of the kind evaluated in the paper.

The minimal nonidentity adversary emits one scalar response per public
attribute, all zero, and otherwise performs constant public work and one
$H_0$ evaluation. It makes no $H_1$ query and no pairing. Its cost is linear
only in the unavoidable output length. The strengthened form uses one $H_1$
evaluation and one public scalar multiplication per attribute, still with one
$H_0$ query. Both are far below the campaign's $2^{45}$ practical-work cutoff.

The adversary makes zero calls to the key-generation and signing oracles. The
chosen output message has therefore never appeared in a signing query, and no
queried key policy can violate the game's restriction. A public random-oracle
query that contains the chosen message is part of ordinary Fiat--Shamir
signing and is not a signing-oracle query.

\section{Why the proof extraction fails}

Theorem~3 of the target first assumes that an accepted forgery has the honest
algebraic structure of $A$, $B$, and $C$. Figure~1 verification never proves
that premise. After forking, the reduction extracts $k^*t^*$ from the two
$s_\alpha$ responses and $k^*$ from the two $s_k$ responses. It then divides
to calculate $t^*$, computes $(C^*)^{1/t^*}$, and uses a final exponent whose
denominator contains $\delta^*_{\rm id}k^*t^*$.

For the nonidentity forgery, the extracted proof witnesses are
$k^*t^*=\beta=0$ and $k^*=1$, hence $t^*=0$. The values $1/t^*$ and
$1/(\delta^*_{\rm id}k^*t^*)$ do not exist. Indeed, the accepted
$C^*=g_2$ cannot equal an honest $g_2^{r t^*}$ when $t^*=0$. The all-identity
form instead has $k^*=0$, so even the stated calculation of $t^*$ is
undefined. The proof also samples its special $\delta^*_{\rm id}$ from
$\Zp$ before using it in a denominator, rather than excluding zero.

These observations explain why the reduction does not rule out the forgery.
The attack itself needs no appeal to the proof: acceptance follows directly
from the verifier equations.

\section{Experimental evidence and reproducibility}

\subsection{Primary typed-group reproduction}

The primary reproducer implements the exact typed Figure~1 equations in the
standard library. It represents an element of each prime-order cyclic group by
its exponent and maps a pairing of exponents $(a,b)$ to $ab$. That model is
faithful for every group equality used here. Although the representation
contains discrete logarithms internally, no attack function reads or branches
on them. Hash inputs use distinct group tags and explicit length prefixes.

The pinned modulus is the common BN254 scalar order
\[
21888242871839275222246405745257275088548364400416034343698204186575808495617.
\]
This constant is an explicit reproducible instantiation, not a claim that it
matches the unresolved Charm/PBC backend byte for byte. The attack is
algebraically independent of the chosen prime order.

The sealed run covered:
\begin{itemize}
  \item honest all-AND controls at 1, 3, and 100 attributes;
  \item all three attack forms at legal 1-, 3-, and 100-attribute counts,
        plus 0-attribute algebraic edge checks that are not treated as a
        qualifying endpoint;
  \item four separately constructed fresh-message forgeries;
  \item portability of the zero-response transcript across changed attribute

  \item eight negative controls changing the held message, challenge, each of

supports neither an absolute first-ever claim nor novelty for the technique.

Generic group-validation guidance predates this work. RFC 8235 discusses
applications that exclude an identity Schnorr public key~\cite{rfc8235}, and
RFC 9591 specifies identity rejection in a different group interface
~\cite{rfc9591}. Neither analyzes FABS. More decisively, the attack in
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonidentity} transmits generators in all three group slots and
therefore survives identity-only validation of $A$, $B$, and $C$.

Tan, Heng, and Goi analyze the distinct Guo--Zeng ABS construction
~\cite{tanhenggoi}. Zhang et al. analyze the distinct Maji--Prabhakaran--
Rosulek family~\cite{zhangetal}. Those results are relevant ABS history but do
not disclose the public witnesses or Figure~1 cancellation reported here. We
make no novelty claim for Schnorr transcripts, Fiat--Shamir, zero witnesses,
or identity validation.

\section{Impact, repair direction, and limitations}

The attack is a direct universal fresh-message forgery under the target's
ordinary mathematical verifier. It supplies the signing endpoint demanded by
the paper's adaptive EUF-CMA game without recovering the master secret. The
weakness is at the design level: it follows from the printed relation and
works with valid group elements in an ordinary prime-order group.

Rejecting identity values in the transmitted $(A,B,C)$ is not enough. A rule
that rejects the derived $Y'=1$ would be a scheme change, and it would conflict
with honest zero choices permitted by Figure~1 unless signing also changed to
sample suitable values from $\Zp^*$. Such a check may block these transcripts,
but this audit does not claim it is a complete repair.

A robust revision needs a proved statement that binds $(A,B,C)$ to a
credential issued under the master key, binds the same signing values to valid
MSP reconstruction coefficients for the declared attributes, and excludes the
public zero-witness branch. It must define compatible nonzero sampling and a
normative transcript encoding and then receive new correctness,
unforgeability, and implementation-conformance analysis. We have not
validated a drop-in patch.

The result has the following limitations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item it targets Figure~1 KP-ABS unforgeability, not SP-ABS or anonymity;
  \item the retained paper is metadata-matched and hash-pinned, but canonical
        IACR PDF byte identity could not be established;
  \item the paper does not define a normative byte encoding for the
        Fiat--Shamir tuple;
  \item the exact Charm/PBC BN254 backend remains unresolved, so the public
        model makes no author-backend or wire-conformance claim;
  \item the source signer omits general MSP coefficients, but the attack does
        not use or depend on that separate source limitation;
  \item the independent actual-pairing result is a common explicit BN254
        instantiation and makes no Charm/PBC, author-codec, or author-source
        conformance claim; and
  \item the history conclusion is dated and bounded.
\end{itemize}

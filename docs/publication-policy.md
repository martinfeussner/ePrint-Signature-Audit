# Publication policy

An attack is published only after all of the following are supported by recorded
evidence: no prior public attack against the target scheme was located; the weakness
is in the intended design; a claimed full parameter set is affected in practical
time and memory; signing capability is recovered or an efficient fresh-message
forgery is produced; the ordinary verifier accepts; conditions and limitations are
explicit; adversarial review and independent reproduction pass; the final history
search is complete; known methods are credited; and AI provenance is disclosed.
Human verification may add further review, but is not required for verified status.
Where it remains clear and professional, attack-paper titles use scheme-related
wordplay followed by a descriptive subtitle stating the result and scope.
Every attack paper places a prominent AI provenance and review-status disclosure
immediately after the title and before the abstract. The disclosure states when
OpenAI Codex generated and selected the title and its scheme-related wordplay.
It also states that the audit used the OpenAI model Daybreak Blue at the
ultra reasoning level.
The paper byline and PDF author metadata identify Martin Feussner, with the
affiliation `Selmer Center, University of Bergen` and contact address
`martin.feussner@uib.no`, matching the presentation used in the NGCC Signature
Audit. The human byline and the AI provenance disclosure are both retained.

Implementation-only flaws, side channels, faults, toy parameters, proof gaps, generic
distinguishers, or infeasible extrapolations are not promoted as successful attacks.
